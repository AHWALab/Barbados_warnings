This product shows flood potential, not an official warning. Deciding warning levels is the mandate of the national institutions. Classes are defined on maximum unit streamflow in m3/s per km2, mapped as the ensemble 90th percentile, and clustered into zones. View A shows the hazard alone; View B folds in population density and settlement proximity through a likelihood by impact matrix.

Confidence is read from the probability that unit streamflow reaches the first band: HIGH at 50 percent or more, MED 20 to 50, LOW 5 to 20. It controls fill opacity, and low confidence zones are drawn with a dashed edge. A zone must hold at least 3 of 50 members above the first band and cover at least 0.32 km2, the Guatemala minimum zone area. A zone takes the highest class that covers at least a tenth of it, and reports the worst class present separately, so one hot cell cannot set the class of a large zone.

Read this before interpreting.
1. This event is small. The whole island peaks at 1.7 m3/s per km2 in the analysis ensemble and 0.9 in the forecast ensemble, so on the Guatemala bands almost everything falls in No signal or Low. The Barbados calibrated bands (0.25, 0.40, 0.50, 0.70) exist to make the spatial pattern readable in training. Compare the two and note that only the first set is comparable with the Guatemala product.
2. The forecast rainfall grids (qpfaccum) are empty in every member of this run, so only the analysis rainfall (qpeaccum) is offered as a rainfall layer.
3. At the peak cycle, 17 August 14:00, the whole island is one connected signal area of 432 km2, so the calibrated view returns a single zone holding 252,600 people. That is the honest answer for a broad, shallow signal: read the credible signal area with the zone count, and use the per pixel class raster underneath to see the pattern inside the zone.
4. In the flood inundation product, cycle 17 August 15:00 carries rasters on the Saint Michael window rather than Saint Thomas. That defect is in the FIM output of this run and does not affect the fields shown here, which are read from the EF5 ensemble directly.
