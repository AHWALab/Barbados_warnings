This product shows flood potential, not an official warning. Deciding warning levels is the mandate of the national institutions. Classes follow the FLASH scheme on maximum unit streamflow in m3/s per km2, read at the ensemble 90th percentile and clustered into zones. View A shows the hazard alone; View B folds in population density and settlement proximity through a likelihood by impact matrix.

By parish

Confidence is read from the probability that unit streamflow reaches the first band: HIGH at 50 percent or more, MED 20 to 50, LOW 5 to 20. A zone must hold at least 3 of 50 members above the first band and cover at least 0.32 km2, the Guatemala minimum zone area. A zone takes the highest class that covers at least a tenth of it, and reports the worst class present separately.

Read this before interpreting.
1. This is a hurricane. The 24 h rainfall of the ensemble averages 229 mm over the island and every member reaches unit streamflow far above 4 m3/s per km2, so on the FLASH bands the ensemble p90 puts 98 percent of the land in Very high and the credible signal covers the whole island. The zone product returns one island wide zone. That is the correct reading of the bands for an event of this size.
2. The structure of the event is in the continuous fields. Switch the background to the probability of reaching 4, the p90 or the member maximum: the south and west run higher than the north and east, and the 3 km blocks of the WRF rainfall are visible.
3. Every one of the eleven parishes passes the FIM trigger of 1.0 by a wide margin, so the flood inundation product ran for the whole island. The flood maps and the impact forecast carry the spatial detail that the class map cannot.
4. Rainfall entered the model through the analysis path, so the accumulation shown is the IMERG warm up of 16.8 mm plus the 24 h forecast rain of each member.
